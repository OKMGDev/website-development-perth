## Hospitality, creative work and waterfront retail

Fremantle’s mix is distinctive: cafes and restaurants, galleries and studios, independent retail, tour and charter operators, and professional practices that like the port city’s character. Many of those businesses serve both locals and visitors who are deciding in the street or on a phone — hours, menus, whether you take walk-ins, how to book, and whether parking or public transport is realistic.

A hospitality site should make the next action obvious: view the menu, book a table, or check today’s hours. Atmosphere photography helps, but only if pages stay fast. Creative and studio businesses need a portfolio that loads quickly and a short brief form rather than a generic contact dump. Retailers may need a simple catalogue or a full cart, depending on whether people buy in-store, online, or both.

## Two audiences on the same phone

Visitors and residents use Fremantle sites differently, but they share a device. Visitors look for “open now”, location, and whether they need a booking. Residents look for loyalty offers, regular hours, and a reason to return. Trying to write one homepage that sounds like a tourism brochure and a neighbourhood noticeboard usually fails both groups.

A more reliable pattern:

- Lead with what you offer and the next action
- Put hours, address and phone where a thumb can reach them
- Keep seasonal events or markets on a dedicated page you can update without a developer
- Use [website maintenance and support](/services/website-maintenance/) so menus, hours and event copy do not go stale

[UX/UI design](/services/ux-ui-design/) is the right place to settle those journeys before visual styling. If Instagram currently does all the work, the website still needs to catch people who never see the feed — especially for bookings, private events and wholesale enquiries.

## Performance, photography and heritage character

Fremantle brands often want warmth, texture and photography. That is reasonable. It is also how sites become slow. Compress images, avoid autoplaying video in the hero, and treat performance as a launch requirement rather than a later tidy-up. A [website redesign](/services/website-redesign/) is often the moment to keep the character and drop the weight. Our [website redesign checklist](/blog/website-redesign-checklist/) covers what to audit before anyone opens a design file.

Booking widgets, gift-voucher flows and multi-location hours (Fremantle plus a second venue) should be scoped explicitly. They are useful when they match how you actually take bookings; they are expensive clutter when staff still manage everything by phone.
