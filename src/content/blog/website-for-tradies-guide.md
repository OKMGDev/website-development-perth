Tradie websites work when a busy customer can see what you do, where you work and how to ask for a quote in under a minute. Stock photos of hard hats and a paragraph about “quality workmanship” do not substitute for services, proof and a form that survives a thumb on a dusty phone.

This is a structure guide. For sector workflow in projects, see [tradies and construction](/industries/tradies-construction/). The build is usually [custom website design](/services/custom-website-design/) plus focused [landing pages](/services/landing-page-design/) for high-intent jobs.

## Direct answer

A good Perth tradie site leads with the trade and job types, shows real completed work, states suburbs or corridors honestly, and makes calling or requesting a quote obvious on mobile. It does not hide behind a slider, a vague “our services” blob, or a contact page that only offers an email address. Referrals still matter; the site’s job is to confirm you are real, licensed, and will not ignore a Tuesday-evening enquiry from a flooded laundry.

## What visitors are trying to decide

People hiring a plumber, electrician, builder or landscaper are rarely browsing for inspiration. They want:

- Do you do *this* job (not a cousin of it)?
- Do you come to *this* suburb?
- Have you done work that looks like mine?
- How do I ask for a quote without playing phone tag?
- Are you a proper business (ABN, insurance, licences) or a Facebook ghost?

If the homepage cannot answer the first three, the rest of the site will not save the visit. Inner-metro and corridor work look different on the map — [Midland](/locations/midland/), [Osborne Park](/locations/osborne-park/) and [Rockingham](/locations/rockingham/) are not interchangeable service areas — but the decision pattern is the same.

## Page structure that earns the quote

Keep the information architecture boring and useful.

**Home.** What you do, who you do it for, proof (photos or project types), service-area summary, and a quote path. One primary action: call or request a quote. Secondary action can be “see services”.

**Service pages.** One page per job type you actually sell: emergency plumbing, switchboard upgrades, bathroom renovations, roof restoration — *your* list, not a copied directory. Say what the job includes, what you need from the customer (photos, access, age of the property), and what happens after they submit.

**Proof.** A project or gallery pattern with the trade visible, captioned. Before-and-after only when both images are yours. If a client asked for privacy, show close detail you *can* share. Invented testimonials and stock “happy couple with keys” photos train people to distrust you. Linking to reviews you already have is cleaner than copying them into a widget you will not maintain. [Our work](/work/) is how we show web projects; your site should show trade jobs the same way — problem, what you did, where — without invented percentages.

**About.** Team, licences and insurance as facts. “Family owned” only helps if the rest of the site is specific.

**Service areas.** Corridors you cover and, just as important, where you do not.

**Quote or contact.** Mobile-first form plus tap-to-call. If after-hours is emergencies only, say so.

You do not need a blog to launch. You need this skeleton.

## Service areas: be honest or pay for it

“We service all of Perth” is only useful if it is true on a Friday afternoon in peak traffic. Southern-corridor operators should name [Rockingham](/locations/rockingham/) and the outliers they still take; east-heavy firms should name [Midland](/locations/midland/); commercial work often belongs around [Osborne Park](/locations/osborne-park/), not a lifestyle suburb list. False coverage wastes call-outs. Duplicate suburb paragraphs waste the page. Match the copy to how you actually dispatch vans.

## Quote friction on mobile

Assume the visit happens in a hallway, a driveway or a job site, one-handed.

- Sticky call button that does not cover the form
- Form fields: name, phone, suburb, job type, short description, photo upload if your process uses photos
- Job-type dropdown that matches your service pages
- Confirmation of what happens next (who calls, roughly when)
- No mandatory “message” essays
- Errors that make sense when the suburb field is empty

If a campaign is running for one job — for example storm repairs or hot-water replacements — a dedicated [landing page](/services/landing-page-design/) should repeat that job, proof and form without sending people hunting through the whole site.

After-hours: if the form goes to an inbox nobody reads until morning, say so. Surprise silence is how you lose the job to whoever answered.

## Trust, licences and claims

State what is true: licence numbers, insurance as a fact, memberships that still exist. Do not imply guarantees you will not put in a contract. Do not copy competitor claims. [SEO](/services/seo-services/) for trades is mostly clear service pages, real suburb coverage, fast mobile performance and titles that match the job. It is not stuffing “electrician Perth” into every heading.

If you also sell to builders or commercial managers, give them a way to send scope documents or request a trade account path. Mixing DIY emergency copy with tender copy on one page helps neither audience. Two templates can share a design system without sharing a speech.

## Launch checklist

Use this before you pay a deposit or sign off a homepage.

- Homepage states trades and job types in the first screen on a phone
- Each paying service has its own page
- Proof is real and captioned
- Service area matches dispatch reality
- Tap-to-call works
- Quote form works on a small phone, including photo upload if you asked for it
- ABN and licence details findable without a treasure hunt
- Hours and emergency rules are explicit
- Old site URLs redirect if you are replacing a site
- Someone on your team can update photos and job lists without a developer

Content is still the usual delay — photos, licence scans, a final service list. [How long a website project takes](/blog/website-project-timeline/) applies: the build is rarely what stalls a tradie site. ## Common ways tradie sites fail

A homepage paragraph plus a Facebook link; services as unexplained icons; unlabelled galleries; a form with no phone number; stock imagery; pop-ups that block the call button. That is a clarity problem, not a rebrand. A [redesign](/services/website-redesign/) should keep pages that already bring calls and replace the rest with the structure above.
