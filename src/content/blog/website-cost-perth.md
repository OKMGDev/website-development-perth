Website cost in Perth varies because projects vary. A focused brochure site is a different job from a multi-location service platform or a commerce catalogue. There is no honest single figure, and published “average prices” are usually marketing, not research. What you can do is learn the drivers, read a quote’s assumptions, and tell a cheap offer from a thin one.

This article is a buying guide. For a number that matches *your* scope, [request a quote](/contact/) with goals, examples and constraints. Delivery still follows a written [process](/process/) so the price is attached to work, not a vibe.

## Direct answer

You pay for unique templates, integrations, content labour, risk (migrations, redirects, accessibility, performance) and the time to train someone to run the site. Page count is a weak proxy. Ten pages that share one layout are not ten times one page. Three templates plus a booking integration plus a messy content migration can outrank a larger brochure in effort.

[Custom website design](/services/custom-website-design/) and [website redesign](/services/website-redesign/) sit at different risk levels even when the visual ambition looks similar. Redesign must also protect what already ranks and convert existing journeys. A new site has fewer ghosts — and fewer excuses.

## What usually drives the price

Treat these as the real line items, whether or not the quote names them that way.

**Unique templates, not pages.** Home, service, location, article, team, product — each distinct layout is design, build and QA. Repeating a pattern is cheaper than inventing a new one.

**Information architecture.** A plumber with four services is not the same job as a clinic with practitioners, locations and treatments. [Tradie sites](/blog/website-for-tradies-guide/) and [professional services](/industries/professional-services/) sites fail in different ways; both still need a map before visual design.

**Content writing and asset preparation.** If copy, photos and logos are not ready, someone writes, shoots, crops or hunts them. Quotes that assume “content supplied” are not cheaper. They are incomplete until that assumption is true.

**Integrations.** Forms to your inbox are not the same as a CRM, booking tool, membership or inventory feed. Each connection needs mapping, testing and an owner when it breaks.

**Migrations, redirects and SEO protection.** Moving hosts or platforms is [website migration](/services/website-migration/) work: URLs, metadata, files, and a redirect map. Skipping that work is how “a new site” becomes a drop in search traffic.

**Accessibility, performance and QA.** Keyboard paths, forms on real phones, alt text, contrast, and pages that do not stall on a WA mobile connection. This is labour. Omitting it is how cheap builds feel cheap.

**CMS training and documentation.** A site nobody can edit will return as paid change requests. Training is part of launch, not a favour.

**Stakeholder overhead.** Two reviewers who decide quickly cost less than a committee that rediscovers the offer every fortnight. Calendar risk shows up in [project timelines](/blog/website-project-timeline/) and in price when agencies pad for it — or in variations when they do not.

Platform choice is a driver, not the driver. Shopify versus WooCommerce, or Webflow versus WordPress, changes hosting and build shape. It does not erase catalogue cleanup or copy. See [Shopify vs WooCommerce](/blog/shopify-vs-woocommerce/) and [Webflow vs WordPress](/blog/webflow-vs-wordpress/) for operations, then come back here for how that lands in a quote.

## What “cheap” usually skips

Low quotes are sometimes efficient packaging of a small, honest scope. More often they skip work you will still need:

- Redirects from old URLs
- Form testing on mobile, including error states
- Accessibility beyond a colour palette
- Image compression and real performance work
- CMS roles so staff cannot break layout
- Analytics events that match actual enquiries
- A staging site, so the live site is not the sandbox
- Handover notes
- A window of bug-fix support after launch

None of that is luxury. It is the difference between a brochure that launches and a website you can run. [SEO services](/services/seo-services/) cannot later invent a content model or redirect map that was never built.

If the quote is a single line — “website, including SEO” — you are not looking at a scope. You are looking at a slogan.

## How to evaluate a quote without a made-up benchmark

Ignore round numbers presented as “what Perth businesses pay”. Compare assumptions.

### Quote checklist

- **Inclusions.** Templates, page assembly, integrations, and whether copy and photography are in or out.
- **Exclusions.** Explicit is healthier than implied. Hosting, licences, stock photos, print, and third-party app fees should be named.
- **Content responsibility.** Who writes, who approves, what happens if assets arrive late.
- **SEO meaning.** Technical foundations and sensible structure, or a vague promise to “do SEO”.
- **Migration and redirects.** Listed as a workstream, or hoped.
- **QA.** Devices, browsers, accessibility, forms, redirects — mentioned or not.
- **Training.** Session, recording, or “you will figure it out”.
- **Post-launch.** Bug-fix window versus a [maintenance](/services/website-maintenance/) plan versus nothing.
- **Variation rules.** How new templates, extra locations or “can we just add a shop” get priced.
- **People.** Named roles beat an anonymous “team”.

Ask vendors to price the same written brief. If one number is dramatically lower, it is usually a narrower brief, not a bargain. Examples in [our work](/work/) show range of ambition; they are not a price list.

### Questions worth asking

- What did you assume we will supply on day one?
- Which templates are unique?
- What happens to our current URLs?
- Who hosts, and whose name is on the account?
- What is out of scope if we add online booking later?

You are not being difficult. You are stopping two businesses from agreeing to different projects.

## Redesign, campaigns and software are different products

A visual overhaul of a site that already converts some traffic is [redesign](/services/website-redesign/) work. A single-offer campaign page is [landing page design](/services/landing-page-design/) — usually a smaller, tighter scope. A client portal, calculator or custom quoting tool is [web application development](/services/web-application-development/), which has a different risk profile to a marketing site.

Bundling those into one “website” line is how quotes balloon. Split the jobs, even if one team delivers all of them.

Industry and geography change content effort, not a secret suburb tariff. A [Joondalup](/locations/joondalup/) service business that covers the northern corridor needs honest service-area pages. A [hospitality](/industries/hospitality-restaurants/) site needs menu and hours workflows. Those are scope facts. They should appear as templates and content tasks, not as a mysterious loading.

## Maintenance is part of the real cost

Launch is not the end of spending. Platforms, plugins, apps and content change. A care plan for updates, backups and small fixes is usually cheaper than emergency rescues, and it keeps the original investment usable.

Host licences, domains, email, premium plugins and payment fees sit outside design. A quote that is silent on them is not cheaper. You will meet those invoices anyway. Budget for running the site the way you budget for running a vehicle: the purchase is not the only cost.

## How to brief so the quote can be real

Send more than “we need a new website”. Include:

- Business goals (enquiries, bookings, store, recruiting — pick the actual ones)
- Who will edit the site
- Current URL list if you have a site
- Integrations you already pay for
- Examples you like and dislike, with reasons
- Hard constraints (launch event, brand guidelines, accessibility policy)
- Whether content exists or must be written

Suburb context helps when it is operational. Businesses around [Osborne Park](/locations/osborne-park/) often need practical B2B enquiry paths more than lifestyle branding. Say so. It changes templates.
