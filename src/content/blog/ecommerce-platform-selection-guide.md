The right ecommerce platform is the one that matches how you actually sell, fulfil and administer orders — not the one with the loudest ads. **Start with operations, then pick a cart.** Feature checklists come last, because every major platform can “do” products, payments and shipping on paper.

This is the wider map: hosted storefronts, WordPress commerce, builders, custom or headless, and marketplaces versus your own store. When the remaining decision is Shopify versus WooCommerce, use the [Shopify vs WooCommerce guide](/blog/shopify-vs-woocommerce/) — that article owns the two-way comparison.

This is not a [website cost](/blog/website-cost-perth/) article. Get operational fit right before you argue fees.

## Questions to answer before you name a platform

Write short answers. If you cannot, you are not ready to choose software.

1. **What is for sale?** A few signature products, a large catalogue, variants (size/colour), made-to-order, bookings with deposits, wholesale, subscriptions, or a mix.
2. **Who buys?** Local pickup in Perth, Australia-wide shipping, both, or B2B accounts with price lists.
3. **Who runs the store day to day?** You, a shop manager, a marketing person, or an agency? Be honest about technical comfort.
4. **Where does inventory live?** Spreadsheets, a POS, a warehouse system, or “we will figure it out”.
5. **What must the marketing site do besides sell?** Journal, recipes, services, locations, education — content depth changes the CMS conversation.
6. **What would make you migrate in two years?** If the answer is “we will outgrow this in months”, do not pick it for a short-term saving.

[Retail and ecommerce](/industries/retail-ecommerce/) businesses in Perth also hit WA-specific friction: shipping time and cost expectations, click-and-collect, and not promising next-day anywhere when you fulfil from one warehouse.

## A map of the main options

Treat this as a shortlist filter. Platforms change; your operations should not be rewritten to suit a demo store.

### Hosted commerce (Shopify, BigCommerce, and similar)

**You are renting a commerce engine.** Hosting, PCI burden and many core primitives sit with the vendor. You pay a subscription, plus apps, plus theme work.

**Fits when** you want less server maintenance and a team that should not be patching PHP on a Sunday.

**Watch for** app sprawl, theme limits, and plan costs as you grow. Unusual catalogues or wholesale may need a higher plan, apps, or a different platform.

[Shopify development](/services/shopify-development/) is the hosted path we implement most often for Perth SMEs. BigCommerce can fit some catalogue or B2B models better; we will say so rather than force Shopify.

### WordPress + WooCommerce

**You are owning more of the stack.** WooCommerce is a plugin (with an ecosystem) on WordPress. Hosting, updates, security and performance are yours — or your agency’s.

**Fits when** the marketing site and the store must share one editorial world, you already run WordPress well, or you need store logic that hosted carts make awkward.

**Watch for** plugin quality, cheap hosting, and the maintenance burden. This is why our [Shopify vs WooCommerce](/blog/shopify-vs-woocommerce/) article focuses on operations, not feature bingo.

See [WooCommerce development](/services/woocommerce-development/) when that flexibility is genuinely required.

### All-in-one website builders with commerce (Squarespace, Wix, and similar)

**Fits when** the catalogue is small, design-led, and the owner wants one simple editor.

**Watch for** outgrowing payments, variants, reporting, or SEO control. Many [migration](/services/website-migration/) projects we see are businesses leaving a builder because the shop became the business. Leaving is normal; leaving in a hurry during peak season is painful.

### Custom and headless

**Fits when** the storefront is a product: unusual configurators, tightly coupled ERP, multiple channels with one catalogue, or performance constraints a theme cannot meet. That sits closer to [web application development](/services/web-application-development/) plus a commerce backend than to a theme install.

**Watch for** cost, time, and who maintains it. Custom should earn its complexity. Most Perth SMEs do not need it on day one.

### Marketplaces (Amazon, eBay, Facebook/Instagram shops) versus your own store

Marketplaces are **distribution**. Your own site is **brand, margins, and the customer relationship**. Many retailers need both. A marketplace is not a platform strategy by itself: you still need product data discipline and a site that explains the brand when someone searches your name.

## Australian operational fit (the unglamorous filter)

Whatever you pick, walk through real ops:

- **Payments:** cards, PayPal, and common AU instalment methods if you will actually offer them — only add what finance and support can handle
- **GST and invoices:** tax settings that match how you invoice, including GST-free items if you have them
- **Shipping:** Australia Post / couriers, WA regional surcharges, free-shipping thresholds you can afford, click-and-collect for Perth customers
- **Restricted goods:** age checks, dangerous goods, food — platform and app support varies
- **Support hours:** who answers “where is my order?” and from which inbox

A platform that cannot represent your real shipping rules will train customers to email you for everything.

## Decision framework

Work top to bottom. Stop at the first clear answer.

1. **Is commerce a side feature on a content-heavy site you already want in WordPress?** Lean WooCommerce — then read the [two-way comparison](/blog/shopify-vs-woocommerce/).
2. **Is commerce the product, and you want less server ownership?** Lean a hosted cart, usually Shopify for SMEs we see in Perth.
3. **Is the catalogue or B2B model awkward on Shopify’s standard plans even after an honest app review?** Evaluate BigCommerce or a scoped custom path before you drown in apps.
4. **Is the catalogue tiny and the brand highly visual, with no appetite for ops complexity?** A builder can be a valid v1 — with a written “we will migrate when…” trigger.
5. **Are you selling unique configured goods or running software-like workflows around orders?** Scope custom/headless; do not hack a theme into an application.
6. **Are you mostly testing demand?** Marketplaces or a minimal hosted store beat a six-month custom build.

If you are still on the Shopify/Woo fork after this, stop here and use the comparison article. Do not reopen the whole map.

## CMS choice is a different article

People conflate “what the homepage is built in” with “what the cart is”. You can run marketing on [Webflow](/services/webflow-development/) or [WordPress](/services/wordpress-development/) and still sell on Shopify. Split vs combined is an editorial choice. For CMS trade-offs see [Webflow vs WordPress](/blog/webflow-vs-wordpress/); for design vs build skills see [web design vs web development](/blog/web-design-vs-web-development-perth/).

## Migrations: the cost of being wrong

Moving carts means products, customers, redirects, apps, staff retraining and a messy month of “the old link still works?”. Reduce that risk by:

- Not choosing on a friend’s store that sells a different thing
- Not choosing on theme demos with dummy products
- Budgeting photography, shipping rules and policy pages — not only the theme
- Planning [SEO](/services/seo-services/) URLs for products and collections from the start

If you are already on the wrong cart, treat the move as a [migration](/services/website-migration/) with a redirect map, not a theme swap.
