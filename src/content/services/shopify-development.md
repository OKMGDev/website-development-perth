## Shopify website development for Perth retailers

Shopify development for Perth retailers is about more than a pretty theme. Product discovery, trust signals, shipping expectations and checkout friction decide whether browsers become buyers.

We implement Shopify storefronts that match how you actually sell — catalogues, collections, offers and fulfilment — not a demo store filled with placeholder products. [Retail and ecommerce](/industries/retail-ecommerce/) businesses are the core fit; [wellness](/industries/fitness-wellness/) and [hospitality](/industries/hospitality-restaurants/) product lines use the same merchandising discipline when they sell goods, not only bookings.

## What our Shopify builds cover

Theme setup or customisation (Online Store 2.0 sections where they help); product templates that make variants, specs and proof easy to scan on phones; navigation and collection structure that mirrors how customers shop; apps chosen for a reason (shipping, reviews, subscriptions) — not an app zoo; and Australian-friendly payments and shipping rules where your ops allow. Launch includes redirects if migrating, analytics, forms and QA.

You should leave with collection architecture, product template rules your team can follow, a written app list with why each exists, and checkout tested against how you pack and ship — including GST presentation that matches your accountant’s advice, not a theme default from another country.

## Shopify vs WooCommerce — a decision, not a slogan

Shopify suits most merchants who want a hosted platform with less server overhead. [WooCommerce](/services/woocommerce-development/) fits teams already invested in WordPress who need deeper custom store logic. Read our [Shopify vs WooCommerce guide](/blog/shopify-vs-woocommerce/) if you are still deciding — the wrong cart choice is expensive to undo.

Shopify is a weak fit if you need heavily custom checkout logic the platform will not allow, or if the “store” is really a handful of SKUs that would convert better as a simple catalogue plus enquiry. We would rather send you to a [custom website](/services/custom-website-design/) than force a cart on a quote-led business.

## Catalogue, apps and operational decisions

Before theme polish we settle: how products are grouped; which filters customers need; variant logic (size, flavour, pack); and what must appear above the fold on a phone. Dirty CSV imports become dirty stores. We clean titles, images and collections in staging rather than launching the exporter’s mess.

Apps are a commercial decision. Reviews, subscriptions, wholesale or loyalty only go in when someone on your team will operate them. Each app is a performance and support surface. Theme edits that fight the theme editor are how stores become unmaintainable; we prefer section-based customisation you can still use after handover.

Payments and shipping must match fulfilment: click-and-collect, metro vs regional, free-shipping thresholds, and carriers you actually use. Configuring rates for a warehouse you do not have is how customers get a nasty surprise at checkout.

## Risks, integrations and what we measure

Risks we plan for: app conflicts at checkout; a theme update that overwrites custom CSS; missing 301s after a [migration](/services/website-migration/); and product SEO titles that all read like internal SKUs. We do not promise conversion-rate lifts. We do instrument product views, add-to-cart, checkout start and purchase (or the equivalent events your analytics stack supports) so you can see where people leave.

Integrations that often matter: accounting or inventory exports, 3PL or local courier apps, email/SMS for abandoned checkout if you will actually send them, and review platforms with genuine customer content. Pixel and consent setup belongs in launch, not as an afterthought when ads go live.

[SEO](/services/seo-services/) after launch is collection and product content work, not stuffing city names into every title. Duplicate collections and thin tag pages are the usual own-goal.
