## What a studio website has to do that an app does not

A booking app is for people who have already decided. A website is for everyone who has not: the person comparing yoga studios in [Fremantle](/locations/fremantle/), the shift worker checking whether a [Joondalup](/locations/joondalup/) gym has early classes, or the friend who was sent a link after a conversation at work. If the first impression is a login wall or a buried timetable, that visitor leaves and the app never sees them.

Gyms, boutique studios, personal training, Pilates and wellness clinics need the site to make the offer obvious, show what a session feels like, and hand the right person into a trial, membership or casual path.

## Casual, membership and intro-offer journeys

Not every visitor wants the same product. Casual visitors need today’s timetable, a price they can understand, and a low-friction way to try one session. Membership prospects need to see what is included, how onboarding works, and who they will train with — without a 12-tab pricing matrix. Intro offers and “first week” packs need their own page so paid campaigns do not dump cold traffic onto a generic homepage.

Personal training and practitioner-led wellness (for example remedial work that sits beside training) often start with a conversation, not a class pack. Those paths should lead to a consult or intro session enquiry, not a generic “join now” button that assumes everyone is buying the same membership.

Returning members still use the public site for holiday hours, guest visits and class descriptions. If those answers only live in an app, you create extra front-desk questions.

## Timetables, trainers and first-class nerves

Timetable updates are the operational headache every studio knows. The website should not become a second spreadsheet the owner has to keep in sync by hand. Where you already use a timetable or booking product, we usually embed or link it so the source of truth stays in one place. Where the timetable is simple, a CMS block the studio manager can edit may be enough.

Trainer and practitioner profiles do more than fill a “meet the team” grid. First-timers often choose a person, not a brand. Show who teaches which format, whose style is beginner-friendly, and what to bring. Describe a first class as a process — arrival, what happens in the room, how long to allow — without promising body-composition results or other outcomes you cannot control.

## Visual storytelling that still converts

Wellness brands live on photography and atmosphere. That is a strength only if the images match the room people will walk into. Over-produced lifestyle galleries that could belong to any studio in any city waste the chance to show your light, your floor, your neighbourhood.

[Webflow](/services/webflow-development/) is a frequent fit when the brand is visual and editors still need to change campaigns without waiting on a developer. WordPress can also work when the content model is more blog-and-schedule than art-directed landing pages. [Webflow vs WordPress](/blog/webflow-vs-wordpress/) sets out the operational trade-off. Motion and heavy galleries need discipline: a homepage that crawls on a phone outside the studio is a failed first visit.

## Campaign spikes and pages that can keep up

January, Mother’s Day and local event tie-ins create traffic that does not behave like your regular member. Those visitors need a campaign page with one offer and one action — not a tour of the entire site. [Landing page design](/services/landing-page-design/) is how paid and social traffic match the ad, then pass into the booking system you already run.

Seasonal spikes are also when [maintenance](/services/website-maintenance/) matters. Editors need a safe way to swap offers, hide expired packs, and restore the default homepage when the push ends.

## Operations, integrations and phone-first use

Most studio traffic is on a phone: checking whether there is a class tonight, screenshotting an address, or tapping through from Instagram. Click-to-call, sticky booking actions, and a timetable that does not require pinch-zooming are not polish — they are the product.

Integrations we typically plan around (without forcing a particular vendor) include the booking or membership platform you already pay for, email or SMS tools for intro sequences, and analytics events that distinguish “viewed timetable” from “started a trial”. We do not invent membership numbers or transformation stories; proof should be photos, class descriptions, and credentials you already hold.
